What's actually happening
under the hood.

The model

An LSTM neural network trained on daily closing prices. It looks at the last 100 trading days and predicts the next one. For a forecast, that prediction gets fed back in as if it were real, and the model predicts the day after that — repeated for however many days you ask for.

Because each forecast day depends on the model's own earlier guesses, accuracy degrades the further out you look. Treat long forecasts as illustrative, not reliable, and never as financial advice.

How accuracy is measured

The most recent 30% of a ticker's history is held out and never shown to the model during that run. RMSE, MAE, and MAPE describe the average size of the miss between the model's prediction and the real closing price, in price units and in percentage terms. R² describes how much of the price movement the model's predictions explain, on a scale up to 1.0.

Indicators shown alongside the forecast

EMA (exponential moving average) smooths price action, weighting recent days more heavily. RSI (relative strength index) measures how fast and how far price has moved recently, on a 0–100 scale, with readings above 70 or below 30 conventionally read as overbought or oversold. MACD tracks the relationship between two EMAs and is often used to spot momentum shifts. Bollinger bands plot a moving average with bands two standard deviations above and below it, widening and narrowing with volatility.

Data & limits

Price history and headlines come from Yahoo Finance via the yfinance library. Downloads are cached for six hours so repeated runs on the same ticker don't re-fetch data. The model is univariate — it only ever sees closing price, not volume, news, or fundamentals.
